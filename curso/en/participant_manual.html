Installation

  1. Copy the whole PCAPro folder to your computer (do not leave it on a USB stick: it will be slow).
  2. Go into the folder, right-click servidor.ps1 and choose Run with PowerShell.
  3. Your browser will open by itself at http://localhost:8790. If it does not, type that address by hand in Chrome or Edge.
  4. Leave the black PowerShell window open while you work: it is the server. Closing it shuts the application down.
Your data never leaves your computer PCAPro computes everything inside the browser. There is no remote server, nothing is uploaded to the internet and it works offline. You can use it with unpublished or confidential data at no risk whatsoever.
If the port is busy Open PowerShell in the folder and type: powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File servidor.ps1 -Port 9001, then go to http://localhost:9001.

Session 1

Fundamentals and loading the data

4 hours · Block 1 of PCAPro (first part)

1.1 · What PCA is and what it is not

Principal component analysis condenses the information held in many correlated quantitative variables into a few new variables —the principal components— which are linear combinations of the originals, are uncorrelated with one another and are ordered by the amount of variance they retain.

It was formulated by Karl Pearson in 1901 as the problem of finding the line that best fits a cloud of points, and rediscovered by Harold Hotelling in 1933 from the maximisation of variance. Both routes arrive at the same place: the principal axes are the eigenvectors of the covariance or correlation matrix.

The idea in one sentence PCA does not invent information: it reorganises it. The total variance does not change, it is merely redistributed across axes ordered from largest to smallest. If the variables are not correlated, there is nothing to summarise.

What it is used for: reducing dimensions, visualising on a plane what lives in many dimensions, discovering groups of variables that behave together, building composite indices, detecting outliers and generating orthogonal predictors for regression.

What it is NOT used for: PCA is not a hypothesis test and does not set out to separate groups. It is an unsupervised technique: it ignores any treatment variable completely. If your question is “do my treatments differ?”, the tool is a discriminant analysis, a MANOVA or a PERMANOVA, not a PCA.

1.2 · The minimum mathematics you need

We start from a matrix X with n rows (individuals) and p columns (variables). Each column is centred by subtracting its mean and, almost always, divided by its standard deviation. From that the covariance (or correlation) matrix S is computed, and this is solved:

S · vk = λk · vk

  • λk (eigenvalue): the variance captured by component k. They all sum to the total variance; with a correlation matrix that sum is exactly p.
  • vk (eigenvector): the direction of the component.
  • Score of an individual: its coordinate on the new axis.
  • Loading of a variable: its correlation with the component. It is what you interpret.

1.3 · How your data have to be laid out

  • First row: the names of the variables. No leading spaces, no merged cells.
  • Every row after that: one observation (one plant, one plot, one sample).
  • One table per sheet. No blank rows or columns in between, no totals at the bottom.
  • Gaps genuinely empty, or marked as NA. Never with zeros.

2.2 · The two adequacy tests

Bartlett's test of sphericity. It tests whether the correlation matrix is the identity, that is, whether the variables are uncorrelated. You want to reject: p < 0.05. Watch out: with a large n it almost always comes out significant, so it is necessary but not sufficient.

The KMO index. It compares the observed correlations with the partial ones. If the variables share common factors, the partial correlations are small and the KMO high.

Kaiser's scale for the KMO.
KMORatingDecision
≥ 0.90ExcellentGo ahead
0.80 – 0.89MeritoriousGo ahead
0.70 – 0.79AcceptableGo ahead
0.60 – 0.69MediocreWith reservations
0.50 – 0.59LowReview the variables
< 0.50UnacceptableDo not run a PCA

The same calculation variable by variable gives its MSA. Variables with MSA < 0.50 are removed one at a time, recalculating after each removal.

2.3 · Scaling: the most important decision

Rule of thumb If your variables are in different units —and in biology they almost always are— the answer is z standardisation, which amounts to running the PCA on the correlation matrix. You only work unscaled when every variable shares the same unit and their variances are comparable.

Session 3

Extraction and the number of components

4 hours · Block 2 of PCAPro

3.1 · What extraction produces

  • Eigenvalues: how much variance each axis captures.
  • Scores: the new, reduced data set, which you can download and use in another analysis.
  • Loadings: the correlation of each variable with each component.
  • Communality: what proportion of a variable's variance the retained components pick up. Below 0.50, that variable is poorly represented.

3.2 · The eight retention criteria

Criteria computed by PCAPro, ordered by reliability.
CriterionHow it worksBehaviour
Parallel analysis (Horn) Compares your λ with those of hundreds of structureless matrices of the same size The best rated. Very reliable
Velicer's MAP Minimum of the mean squared partial correlationVery good
Broken stick Compares with the random division of a segment broken at randomConservative
Kaiser–Guttman (λ > 1) Retains those that explain more than one variableOverestimates, especially with a large p
Jolliffe (λ > 0.7)A relaxed version of KaiserOverestimates even more
Scree elbowThe point where the fall flattens outVisual, somewhat subjective
Cumulative varianceUp to 70 % or 80 %Never as the only criterion
InterpretabilityYour judgementThe decisive one
How the decision is made in practice Run every criterion, see where they agree and, within that range, pick the number that gives the most interpretable solution. In the paper you write it like this: “k components were retained on the basis of parallel analysis, the scree plot and interpretability, explaining X % of the variance”.

Session 4

Rotation and factor maps

4 hours · Blocks 3 and 4 of PCAPro

4.1 · Why rotate

The axes that come out of a PCA maximise variance, not interpretability. The typical result is that almost every variable loads strongly on PC1 and the remaining components are left as contrasts that are hard to name. Rotation turns the retained axes within the same subspace in search of Thurstone's simple structure: each variable loading strongly on a single component.

What is preserved and what changes Preserved: the total variance of the set of retained components and the communalities.
Changed: how that variance is shared between axes, the loadings, and the order. After rotation the axes no longer run from largest to smallest λ; that is why PCAPro renames them RPC1, RPC2…
The rotations available in PCAPro.
MethodTypeWhen to use it
VarimaxOrthogonalThe default choice. Well-differentiated, independent components
QuartimaxOrthogonalWhen you expect a dominant general factor
Equamax / ParsimaxOrthogonalCompromises; less stable
PromaxObliqueFast; large samples
Oblimin / QuartiminObliqueWhen it is reasonable for the dimensions to be correlated
Orthogonal or oblique? Use an orthogonal rotation if you need independent components or if on theoretical grounds you expect unrelated dimensions. Use an oblique one if it is reasonable for them to be correlated, which is the usual case with biological phenomena. With an oblique rotation you interpret the pattern matrix, and you have to look at Φ: if the largest correlation between components is below 0.15, the obliquity gained you nothing and it is worth going back to varimax.

4.2 · How to read the maps

Cloud of individualsCloud of variables
What is drawnOne point per observationOne vector per variable
What you readDistances: nearby points, similar profilesAngles with respect to the origin
ScaleThat of the scoresAlways between −1 and 1

Rules for the correlation circle: vectors pointing the same way, variables positively correlated; opposite, negative correlation; perpendicular, uncorrelated in that plane. A short vector does not mean the variable is unimportant, but that its information lies on other axes: do not interpret its direction.

cos² measures how well a point is represented (≥ 0.7 very good, 0.5–0.7 acceptable, < 0.3 not interpretable). The contribution measures how much weight that element carries in building the axis; its benchmark is 100/p for variables and 100/n for individuals.

Session 5

Interpretation, report and project

4 hours · Blocks 5 and 6 of PCAPro

5.1 · Naming an axis

  1. Look at the variables with |loading| above your threshold.
  2. Separate the positive ones from the negative ones. If both signs are there, it is a contrast axis.
  3. Look for what they have in common: size? an acquisitive versus a conservative strategy? an environmental gradient?
  4. Give it a short, substantive name. If you cannot name it, you probably should not be retaining it.
  5. Confirm it with the supplementary elements and the extreme individuals.

5.2 · The test value

It answers this: is the centroid of this category further from the origin than you would expect by chance? Under the null hypothesis it follows a standard normal distribution, so |v| ≥ 1.96 is equivalent to p < 0.05 and |v| ≥ 2.58 to p < 0.01. The sign tells you which side of the axis the category falls on.

The warning you must not forget PCAPro lets you compare groups on the components with an ANOVA. That is legitimate because the PCA did not use the grouping variable to build the axes. But: you are running k tests without correcting for multiple comparisons, and the axes were chosen a posteriori for maximising variance, so the p value is optimistic. Use it as description, not as a confirmatory test. If your research question is whether the groups differ, the right answer is a MANOVA, a PERMANOVA or a discriminant analysis on the original variables.

5.3 · Goodness of fit

The retained components let you rebuild the correlation matrix. The difference from the observed one is the residuals, summarised in the RMSR: below 0.05 is a good fit, between 0.05 and 0.08 acceptable, and above 0.08 you are losing structure.

Glossary

Eigenvalue (λ)Variance captured by a component.
EigenvectorDirection of the component in the space of the variables.
Loading (carga)Correlation between a variable and a component. It is what you interpret.
Score (puntuación)Coordinate of an individual on a component.
CommunalityProportion of the variance of a variable explained by the retained components.
cos²Quality of representation of a point on an axis or a plane.
ContributionPercentage share of an element in building an axis.
Simple structureThe ideal situation after rotation: each variable loads on a single component.
KMO / MSASampling adequacy, overall and per variable.
RMSRRoot mean square residual between the observed matrix and the reproduced one.
Test valueDeparture of the centroid of a category from the origin, in standard normal units.
Supplementary elementA variable or an individual projected without having built the axes.

How to cite PCAPro

If you publish a paper, a thesis or a report with results obtained on this platform, cite the software. It is not a formality: it is what keeps the work of maintaining and improving it going, and what lets others trace what your analyses were done with.
