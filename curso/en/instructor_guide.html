Instructor's guide

Principal Component Analysis workshop with PCAPro · 20 hours

Use: Internal document, do not hand out
Companion file: INSTRUCTOR_KEYS.md
Suggested group: 12 to 20 participants
Confidential document It contains the answers to every practical and the assessment criteria. Together with INSTRUCTOR_KEYS.md and the answers section of assessment.html, it must not be handed to the participants.

Preparation beforehand

A week before

  1. Run curso/generar_datos.ps1 and work through the five practicals yourself in PCAPro before you teach them. Check them against INSTRUCTOR_KEYS.md.
  2. Confirm that the laboratory computers allow PowerShell scripts to run. If institutional policy blocks them, test this alternative beforehand: opening index.html with a double click works for everything except loading the example files from datos/en/; in that case hand out the CSV files and have them drag them in.
  3. Prepare a USB stick or a shared folder with: the complete PCAPro folder, the manual as a PDF and the five loose CSV files.
  4. Take the screenshots marked in the manual at the resolution of the classroom projector.

On the day of the workshop

  • Arrive 30 minutes early: setting up on 20 computers always takes longer than planned.
  • Keep a spare computer, already set up, to hand.
  • Write the address http://localhost:8790 on the board: they will ask for it several times.
About the black PowerShell window It is what confuses people most on the first day. Make it clear from minute one: that window is the program. If they close it, the page stops working. Several of them will close it ‘to tidy up the screen’ and will not understand why everything went down.

Mistakes participants typically make

MistakeHow it shows upHow to correct it
Leaving the ID as an active variable A very odd component appears, dominated by the record number Head it off in session 1. Always ask ‘is this a measurement or a label?’
Leaving numeric treatment codes active 1, 2, 3 go in as if they were a continuous scale PCAPro flags them with ‘group?’. Teach them to look for that signal
Not standardising PC1 explains 95% and is dominated by the variable with the largest numbers That is what practical 2 is for. Go back to it when it happens
Confusing a loading with a score They try to interpret the table of scores variable by variable Mnemonic: loadings = variables, scores = individuals
Interpreting short vectors ‘This variable is related to that one’, pointing at two tiny arrows Colour the circle by cos² and set the rule: if it is pale, it is not interpreted
Reading distances in the biplot between points and arrows ‘This individual is close to this variable’ Remind them that the scale of the arrows is arbitrary; what you read is the projection
Retaining too many components They settle for whatever Kaiser says Practical 4 is the vaccine. Refer back to it every time
Interpreting a component they cannot name ‘PC3 is… well, variables 4 and 7’ If it has no name, it is not retained. Be strict about this
Forgetting that the sign is arbitrary They are alarmed because their axis came out ‘the other way round’ from their neighbour's Show that multiplying by −1 changes nothing; it only has to be stated
Concluding that the PCA ‘proved’ differences between treatments In the final report Session 5. It is the most serious conceptual mistake and the most frequent
Leaving the response variable active In the integrated project, with Yield Explain the circularity: if yield builds the axis, you cannot say that the axis explains yield
Closing the PowerShell window ‘My page has gone down’ Warn them about it on the first day and repeat it on the second

Discussion questions

Session 1

  • If PCA does not test hypotheses, what use is it in a thesis? Which section of the paper does it go in?
  • Can a PCA ‘go wrong’? What would that look like?
  • Someone measures 40 variables on 25 individuals. What would you tell them?

Session 2

  • The KMO for seeds is 0.556 and for soils 0.752. Both have a sufficient n. Where does the difference come from?
  • Why is including the area computed from length and width a problem, if it is a variable that really is measured in practice?
  • If you drop Moisture_pct because its MSA is low, are you losing real information? What would you say to a reviewer who questions it?
  • The three outliers in seeds are possible values, not data-entry errors. Would you remove them?

Session 3

  • Kaiser extracted three components from pure noise. Why is it still the most widely used criterion?
  • What would you do if parallel analysis says 2, broken stick says 1 and Kaiser says 4?
  • Is it legitimate to retain one component more than the criteria indicate just so you can draw a plot in the plane?

Session 4

  • If rotation preserves the total variance, what exactly do we gain by rotating?
  • When should you NOT rotate?
  • In your field, is it reasonable for the dimensions to be correlated? Does that point to oblique rotation?

Session 5

  • Two confidence ellipses of the mean do not overlap. Can you conclude that the groups differ? And if they were concentration ellipses?
  • If the PCA did not use the treatment to build the axes, why is an ANOVA on the scores still optimistic?
  • How does what you can claim with a PCA differ from what you can claim with a discriminant analysis?

If participants bring their own data It is what they value most, but it can derail the workshop: formatting problems come up and eat hours. Recommendation: give it only the last hour of session 5, after everyone has finished the integrated project, and say from the start that the analysis of their own data is indicative, not exhaustive.
